# The long-play syllabus

*Draft v0.1 — 24 weeks. Numbering corrected 2026-09-21 (classical 21–22, cinematic 23–24).*
*This is a living document: weeks move as the listening log shows what lands and what bounces. Revision process: `reference/syllabus-revision-process.md`.*

**The learner:** Wesley, 26. Trained ear, advanced classical theory, beginner on
critic vocabulary and music history, curious beginner on production.
**The job:** transfer what he already knows (theory, listening) onto the music he
actually listens to — plus the history and "how" behind it.
**Terminal objective:** read dense, critic-level write-ups fluently. The course is
the ladder; the glossary is the rungs.
**Design law (the Kraftwerk law):** context unlocks appreciation. Never assign a
challenging album without its story first.

~24 weeks. Five albums a week: 1 anchor (canonical text), 3 adventurous (adjacent
readings), 1 wild card (stretch assignment, calibrated to trust — early ones stay
near his taste, get weirder as the log shows absorption). Each week teaches ONE
concept and ONE production technique via the listen-for cue. His island albums
(Wish You Were Here, OK Computer, Ctrl) appear as taught reference texts, never
as new assignments.

---

## Foundations (2 weeks)

**Week 1 — How to listen on purpose.** Objective: learn the course's core move —
one close-listening assignment per album. Anchor: Pink Floyd — The Dark Side of
the Moon (known ground; hear it like the spat lab taught you). Adventurous: Pink
Floyd — Animals, The Smile — A Light for Attracting Attention, Four Tet —
Beautiful Rewind. Wild card: Can — Tago Mago. Concept: **timbre** — tone color;
why an 808 kick and a real kick playing the same pattern are different
instruments. Technique: **reverb** — the sound of the room the music is "in."

**Week 2 — The album as a designed object.** Objective: hear sequencing — why this
song follows that one. Anchor: Radiohead — OK Computer (known; now its
architecture). Adventurous: The Beatles — Abbey Road, Kendrick Lamar — To Pimp a
Butterfly, SZA — SOS. Wild card: Swans — To Be Kind. Concept: **groove and the
pocket** — rhythm is the pattern on paper; groove is humans agreeing how far
behind the beat to sit. Technique: **panning/stereo image** — where each sound
sits left-to-right; headphones required.

## Unit 1 — His canon, formalized (4 weeks)

Prog/psych, art-rock, confessional R&B: names and histories for music he already loves.

**Week 3 — Where Wish You Were Here comes from.** Anchor: King Crimson — In the
Court of the Crimson King (the Big Bang of prog). Adventurous: Pink Floyd —
Animals, Yes — Close to the Edge, Genesis — Selling England by the Pound. Wild
card: Van der Graaf Generator — Pawn Hearts. Concept: **the Barrett story** —
the history his favorite album is built on. Technique: **tape delay** — the how
behind those vast guitar spaces.

**Week 4 — Taking the rock album apart.** Anchor: Radiohead — Kid A (the
electronic turn). Adventurous: Talk Talk — Laughing Stock, Björk — Homogenic,
The Smile — A Light for Attracting Attention. Wild card: Black Midi — Hellfire.
Concept: **krautrock and motorik** — 1970s West Germany's relentless 4/4 pulse;
the well OK Computer drinks from. Technique: **sampling** — the break, the loop,
hip-hop's founding technology.

**Week 5 — Confession as craft.** Anchor: Frank Ocean — Blonde (the door Ctrl
walked through). Adventurous: FKA twigs — MAGDALENE, Solange — A Seat at the
Table, Kelela — Take Me Apart. Wild card: Ethel Cain — Preacher's Daughter.
Concept: **the confessional lineage** — Lauryn Hill's Miseducation to now; songs
as unsent letters. Technique: **vocal layering** — for the harmony nerd; count
how many voices are really singing.

**Week 6 — Texture as an instrument.** Anchor: My Bloody Valentine — Loveless.
Adventurous: Cocteau Twins — Heaven or Las Vegas (his save, now with context),
Slowdive — Souvlaki, Ride — Nowhere. Wild card: Have A Nice Life —
Deathconsciousness (his save; bleakness as texture). Concept: **shoegaze** —
staring at pedals, not the crowd; guitar as weather. Technique: **distortion as
texture** — blur, not aggression.

## Unit 2 — Electronic, melodic to weird (4 weeks)

**Week 7 — Melodic electronic, formalized.** Anchor: ODESZA — A Moment Apart
(known). Adventurous: Jamie xx — In Colour, Disclosure — Settle, Four Tet —
There Is Love in You. Wild card: Burial — Untrue (melody through fog). Concept:
**build and drop as form** — transfer moment: it's a crescendo and cadence in
different clothes. Technique: **sidechain compression** — the "pumping" under
every EDM kick (and where compression finally gets taught properly — not EQ).

**Week 8 — Kraftwerk week.** Anchor: Kraftwerk — Trans-Europe Express ("Europe
Endless" is track one — the redemption text). Adventurous: Neu! — Neu! 2, Can —
Ege Bamyasi, Tangerine Dream — Phaedra. Wild card: Suicide — Suicide (synths as
punk menace). Concept: **context as appreciation** — 1977 Düsseldorf inventing
the future with machines; the Kraftwerk law in action. Technique: **sequencers
and synthesizers** — pop built note-by-note from circuits.

**Week 9 — Ambient: music for conditions.** Anchor: Brian Eno — Ambient 1: Music
for Airports. Adventurous: Aphex Twin — Selected Ambient Works 85–92, Tim Hecker
— Ravedeath, 1972, Stars of the Lid — And Their Refinement of the Decline. Wild
card: Oneohtrix Point Never — R Plus Seven. Concept: **furniture music** —
transfer moment: Satie's "musique d'ameublement" (1917), reinvented by Eno.
Technique: **tape loops and generative systems** — music that plays itself.

**Week 10 — Weird electronic.** Anchor: SOPHIE — Oil of Every Pearl's Un-Insides.
Adventurous: Arca — Kick I, Flying Lotus — Cosmogramma, Iglooghost — Neo Wax
Bloom. Wild card: 100 gecs — 1000 gecs (maximum discomfort, maximum signal).
Concept: **sound design as composition** — when the new noise IS the piece.
Technique: **synthesis basics** — oscillators and filters; the literal "how."

## Unit 3 — Jazz, fusion doorway in (3 weeks)

Rule: mood doorways, never the avant-garde first. His theory transfers hardest
here — jazz harmony is ii-V-I chains and modes in street clothes.

**Week 11 — The fusion doorway.** Anchor: Weather Report — Heavy Weather.
Adventurous: Herbie Hancock — Head Hunters, Mahavishnu Orchestra — The Inner
Mounting Flame, Snarky Puppy — We Like It Here (drums for the percussionist).
Wild card: Jaco Pastorius — Jaco Pastorius (bass as lead voice). Concept:
**fusion** — jazz harmony + rock energy; follow the ii-V-I's under the
distortion. Technique: **follow one instrument per spin** — the jazz listening
instruction; start with the drums.

**Week 12 — The Miles doorway.** Anchor: Miles Davis — Kind of Blue.
Adventurous: Miles Davis — In a Silent Way, John Coltrane — Blue Train,
Cannonball Adderley — Somethin' Else. Wild card: Miles Davis — Bitches Brew
(the wild one — context first). Concept: **modal playing** — transfer moment:
"So What" is sixteen bars of D dorian; he knows the mode, now he hears what it
frees. Technique: **the room as instrument** — a converted church; reverb
revisited with new ears.

**Week 13 — Coltrane and the album as statement.** Anchor: John Coltrane — A Love
Supreme. Adventurous: Charles Mingus — Mingus Ah Um, Bill Evans — Waltz for
Debby (piano trio — for the pianist), Alice Coltrane — Journey in Satchidananda
(a bridge back to ambient). Wild card: Kamasi Washington — The Epic. Concept:
**the suite** — transfer moment: four movements, one arc; he knows this shape.
Technique: **recorded live, no fixes** — a jazz session vs. a pop one.

## Unit 4 — Songwriters and the voice (3 weeks)

**Week 14 — The confessional songwriter.** Anchor: Jeff Buckley — Grace (for the
"Lover, You Should've Come Over" obsessive). Adventurous: Nick Drake — Pink
Moon, Elliott Smith — Either/Or, Phoebe Bridgers — Punisher. Wild card: Lingua
Ignota — CALIGULA. Concept: **vocal dynamics** — whisper to wail as
compositional range. Technique: **close-mic'ing** — intimacy as a production
decision.

**Week 15 — Harmony nerd week.** Anchor: The Beach Boys — Pet Sounds.
Adventurous: Fleet Foxes — Helplessness Blues, Bon Iver — Bon Iver, The Beatles
— Abbey Road. Wild card: Dirty Projectors — Bitte Orca (harmonies that refuse
to resolve). Concept: **vocal arrangement** — transfer moment: stacking voices
like arranging for wind ensemble. Technique: **multitrack tape** — how one room
becomes a choir.

**Week 16 — The piano songwriter.** Anchor: Joni Mitchell — Blue. Adventurous:
Sufjan Stevens — Carrie & Lowell, Laura Marling — Song for Our Daughter, Big
Thief — U.F.O.F. Wild card: Fiona Apple — Fetch the Bolt Cutters. Concept:
**alternate tunings** — Joni's fifty tunings; tuning as composition. Technique:
**dry recording** — zero reverb; what intimacy costs.

## Unit 5 — Heavy alternative (2 weeks)

**Week 17 — Alt-metal doorway.** Anchor: Deftones — White Pony (for the
discography diver). Adventurous: Tool — Lateralus, Mastodon — Crack the Skye,
Sleep Token — Take Me Back to Eden. Wild card: Have A Nice Life —
Deathconsciousness (if unused in Week 6). Concept: **dynamics as heaviness** —
transfer moment: quiet-to-loud is orchestral thinking. Technique: **odd meters**
— Tool's 5s and 7s; counting as a listening game.

**Week 18 — Heavy as texture.** Anchor: Alcest — Écailles de Lune (blackgaze:
shoegaze + black metal). Adventurous: Deafheaven — Sunbather, Chelsea Wolfe —
Abyss, Boris — Flood. Wild card: Swans — To Be Kind (if unused in Week 2).
Concept: **heaviness as texture, not aggression** — Week 6's blur with the gain
past ten. Technique: **lo-fi as aesthetic** — when bad recording is the point.

Rule: sell the era and the world, not the tracklist. Ruling (Wesley, 2026-9-20):

**Week 19 — The Red Velvet doorway.** Anchor: Red Velvet — Perfect Velvet (the

DALL. Wild card: NewJeans — Get Up (the minimalist disruption). Concept: **the
Red/Velvet duality** — bright pop vs. smooth R&B as a group identity. Technique:

**Week 20 — The industry as context.** Anchor: BTS — Love Yourself: Tear (the

With a Hammer, Crush — From Midnight to Sunrise. Wild card: the LOONA lore
deep-dive (the fandom rabbit hole as assignment). Concept: **the idol system** —
training, concept cycles; the industry story changes the music (cf. Barrett,
Week 3). Technique: **vocal production stacks** — the how behind the polish.

## Unit 7 — Classical: the repertoire you never got assigned (2 weeks)

For the MTAC-panel pianist who knows the exam canon cold. Not "classical 101" —
the classical he never got assigned, plus the thing exam culture never taught:
in classical music the composition is fixed, so the *performance* is the text.
Karajan and Bernstein conducting the same Beethoven are two different albums.

**Week 21 — Orchestral: the riot and the regime.** Anchor: Stravinsky — The Rite
of Spring (the 1913 premiere riot; the Kraftwerk law — once you know the
context, the dissonance lands differently). Adventurous: Shostakovich —
Symphony No. 5 (music under Stalin; the audience wept because they understood),
Mahler — Symphony No. 5, Ligeti — Atmosphères / Requiem (the 2001: A Space
Odyssey bridge into film scores). Wild card: Messiaen — Quartet for the End of
Time (composed in a WWII POW camp). Concept: **interpretation as the text** —
the same score, different performances, different pieces. Technique: **the hall
as studio** — what a recording chooses to put you "inside."

**Week 22 — Solo piano beyond the exam room.** Anchor: Bach — Goldberg
Variations, Glenn Gould's 1981 recording (the interpretation debate — Gould
hums along; pianists still argue about it). Adventurous: Ligeti — Études (for
the technician who played Liszt), Ravel — Gaspard de la nuit, Chopin — the
Ballades (his repertoire, heard as an adult listener instead of an exam
candidate). Wild card: Rzewski — The People United Will Never Be Defeated!
(36 variations, political context). Concept: **the recording is the album** —
why classical listeners argue about *which* Goldberg Variations. Technique:
**close vs. distant mic'ing** — Gould's piano in your lap vs. a concert grand
across a hall.

## Unit 8 — Cinematic scores (2 weeks)

Rule: scores as standalone mood records; the composer as auteur.

**Week 23 — The Zimmer week.** Anchor: Hans Zimmer — Interstellar (OST; the
organ). Adventurous: Hans Zimmer — Dune (OST; his save), Ludwig Göransson —
Oppenheimer (OST), Hildur Guðnadóttir — Chernobyl (OST). Wild card: Mica Levi —
Under the Skin (OST; the avant-score). Concept: **leitmotif** — transfer moment:
Wagner's invention, now Hollywood's grammar. Technique: **the Shepard tone** —
the endlessly rising pitch.

**Week 24 — The composer as auteur.** Anchor: Ryuichi Sakamoto — async (his
final album). Adventurous: Max Richter — Sleep, Joe Hisaishi — Spirited Away
(OST), Sakamoto — Merry Christmas Mr. Lawrence (OST). Wild card: Oneohtrix Point
Never — Uncut Gems (OST; for the OPN fan). Concept: **ma (negative space)** —
silence as a composed element. Technique: **prepared piano** — screws, felt, and
fingers on strings.

**Capstone:** re-read the v1 dense write-ups. If they read clearly, the course worked.

## Glossary seed

From the diagnostic — one line each, Wesley-style. Grows weekly; each write-up
adds at most one term.

- **Timbre** — tone color: what makes a trumpet and a violin on the same note sound different.
- **Groove** — rhythm played by humans: the micro push-and-pull against perfect time that moves your body.
- **The pocket** — every player agreeing how far behind the beat to sit. The groove locks; that's the pocket.
- **Reverb** — thousands of tiny reflections smeared into one wash; the room the music is "in."
- **Echo/delay** — distinct repeats, each quieter; reverb's sharper cousin.
- **Compression** — automatic volume-riding: squashes loud parts so quiet parts come up. NOT EQ.
- **EQ** — tone knobs per frequency. Compression controls volume; EQ controls tone.
- **Sidechain compression** — the "pumping" in dance music: the kick ducks everything else, four times a bar.
- **Krautrock** — 1970s West Germany (Can, Neu!, Kraftwerk): long, hypnotic, driving music.
- **Motorik** — the krautrock beat: relentless 4/4, like driving on the autobahn.
- **Shoegaze** — late-80s UK bands staring at pedals, not the crowd; guitar as weather.
- **The break** — the drum-only stretch of a funk record; Bronx DJs looped it, and hip-hop was born.
- **B-boys / b-girls** — the Bronx dancers who went off during the break.
- **Sampling** — building new music from fragments of old recordings; hip-hop's founding tech.
- **Leitmotif** — a theme attached to a character or idea (Wagner's invention); it transforms as the story does.
- **Modes (e.g. Dorian)** — scales with their own moods; "So What" is sixteen bars of D dorian.
- **Authentic cadence** — V→I, the "amen" ending; every pop song's finality wearing jeans.
- **Multitrack tape** — recording each part separately so they stack; how one room becomes a choir.

## Assessment plan

- **Weekly feedback = formative assessment.** Played, skipped, loved, bounced off —
logged in the listening log. Wild-card reactions count double (discomfort is the
highest-signal feedback).
- **"Can you hear X now?" check-ins** every ~4 weeks, tied to that month's
concepts. Five minutes, conversational, in chat. Missed concepts get re-taught in
a new context — spaced repetition, never a lecture.
- **The listening log is the transcript.** Every album, write-up, reaction, and
concept taught. Reviewable any time.
- **Wild-card calibration.** Early wild cards stay near taste; they get weirder
only as the log shows absorption. A bounced wild card is data, not failure.
- **Graduation criteria:** (1) paraphrases a v1 dense write-up in his own words;
(2) uses 15+ glossary terms unprompted in feedback; (3) names a production
technique on a blind listen; (4) the capstone — the original dense write-ups read clearly.
