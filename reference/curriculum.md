# Curriculum: why a course, not a feed

long-play is a long-horizon personal music education. Basic education
principles apply: assess first, then teach.

## The model

- **Diagnostic assessment first.** Map what the learner knows before the
  syllabus exists: taste, genre/history knowledge, critical vocabulary,
  listening skills, gaps and curiosities. No wrong answers — it maps the
  curriculum.
- **Syllabus with units and learning objectives.** Sequenced like a university
  course: foundations of listening first, then units by genre and history.
  Each unit has concepts, vocabulary, and canonical texts.
- **The 5 weekly albums are course material.** Anchor = the canonical text.
  Adventurous picks = adjacent readings. Wild card = the stretch assignment,
  calibrated to trust — early ones stay near taste, get weirder as the log
  shows absorption. Every pick serves a learning objective, not just a taste
  slot.
- **Scaffolding.** Simple → complex. Each write-up teaches exactly one new
  concept or term; the running glossary is the vocabulary curriculum.
- **Formative assessment, not exams.** Played / skipped / loved / bounced off —
  logged per album. Wild-card reactions count double (discomfort is the
  highest-signal feedback). Periodic check-ins: "can you hear X now?"
- **Spaced repetition.** Concepts reappear across weeks in new contexts —
  reverb in week 1 returns as "the room as instrument" in week 12.
- **The "listen for" cue is the core pedagogical device.** One close-listening
  assignment per write-up trains the ear the way formal courses do.

## The learner this was built for

Trained ear, advanced classical theory (solo piano), beginner on critic
vocabulary and music history, curious beginner on production. The gap is
**transfer**: attaching theory to the pop, electronic, and R&B actually being
listened to. Four pillars: (1) theory-to-ear transfer, (2) production
techniques — the "how," (3) context/history, (4) vocabulary to critic-fluency.

## Terminal objective

Read dense, critic-level music writing fluently. The course is the ladder; the
glossary is the rungs. Graduation: (1) paraphrase a dense write-up in your own
words; (2) use 15+ glossary terms unprompted; (3) name a production technique
on a blind listen; (4) the capstone — the original dense write-ups read clearly.

## Coursework ledger

A permanent log of every write-up and every album: assigned, played, skipped,
reactions, concepts taught. The transcript. Wild-card calibration runs off it:
early wild cards stay near taste and get weirder only as the log shows
absorption. A bounced wild card is data, not failure.
