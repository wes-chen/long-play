# Write-up template

The fill-in guide for every album introduction in the Monday digest. One job:
convert a non-insider into pressing play. Never demonstrate expertise, never
lead with credentials.

## The template (fill every slot; ~180–220 words)

```
[HOOK — 1–2 sentences. A promise, a reveal, or one concrete sonic image with
a fresh analogy. Never credentials, never a score.]

[VERDICT — one line: what this album *does*, and the role it fills this week
(anchor / adventurous / wild card) in plain words.]

▶ Play: [links]

Scores: [number] + [one-line identity for the source, so a non-insider knows
why it matters]. [Consensus note if useful — year-end-list consensus beats
any single number.]

The moment: [2–3 sentences of history — why this record exists *when* it does.]

Lineage: [1–2 sentences. Adjective-first references — the sentence must still
work if the reader has never heard the reference.]

Then vs now: [how reception changed since release.]

Listen for: [one concrete cue. The one thing to track across the record — a
timbre, a room, a hi-hat, a voice.]

Why you: [tie it to the slot in plain words — why this one, this week.]
```

## Readability rules

**Jargon policy.** One technical term per paragraph, max — immediately followed
by a physical/sensory image. Bad: "explores microtonality." Good: "explores
microtonality — notes bent between the keys, like singing slightly out of
tune on purpose until it feels right."

**Reference policy.** Adjective-first, name-second. Never name-drop cold.
Describe the reference's *sound* while naming it. If you haven't heard the
reference either, say so — conversion stories ("I didn't get it, then I did")
beat pronouncements.

**Score-framing.** Scores answer "should I press play?" — never lead with
them. Give the source a one-line identity. RYM aggregates are social proof,
not argument — and RYM skews rockist, so its hip-hop/R&B consensus is less
trustworthy.

**Prose.** Short paragraphs, max ~3 sentences. Verbs, not adjectives —
describe what the music *does*, not what it *is*. Every section earns its
place or it's cut.

## Citation policy (hard rule)

Every factual claim gets a source — Wesley verifies claims, so nothing
asserted without provenance. The fact-bearing slots are Scores, The moment,
Lineage, and Then vs now. Each factual claim in them carries an inline named
link on the claim itself, e.g. `[recorded in a barn over two weeks in
1969](https://…)`. Each album ends with one compact line:

Sources: [Name](url) · [Name](url) · [Name](url)

One source per claim, 2–4 per album, reputable press or better (his bar:
academia > reputable press > blogs; primary sources — liner notes, artist
interviews — beat secondary). If a fact can't be sourced, cut it or mark it
"unverified" explicitly, never present it as settled. Scores already carry a
source identity; the link makes it clickable. Opinion and the listen-for cue
need no citation — but if the listen-for cue asserts a fact (a recording
technique, a personnel claim), cite it. The Feed mirror carries the same
links; citations are part of the write-up, not decoration.

**Verification (worker duty, ADV-LP-06).** A plausible-looking URL is not a source. Before delivery, the digest worker spot-checks the 2–3 most load-bearing factual claims per album: fetch each cited URL, confirm HTTP 200, and confirm the claim (or a close paraphrase) appears on the page. Claims that fail are dropped or marked "unverified" — never shipped as settled. Under time pressure the rational failure mode is satisficing with fake citations; one caught 404 burns the credibility of all the others, so this check is not optional.

## Layering

- **Skim** (most readers stop here; the whole pitch): hook → verdict → play
  links immediately, above the fold.
- **Deep** (opt-in, for the converted): the moment → lineage → then-vs-now →
  the listen-for cue.

## Example hooks

1. **The reveal:** "The most futuristic R&B album of 2017 was recorded to
   sound unfinished on purpose. The cracks are the point."
2. **Direct address + permission:** "Never got into ambient? This is the one
   record that makes the whole genre click — here's the 40-minute version."
3. **The personal scene:** "This is a 3am album. Not metaphorically — it's
   mixed for the hour when you're too tired to perform being okay."

## Genre notes

Match the intro culture of the genre, not the template's default:

- **Hip-hop / R&B:** lead with artist narrative and cultural context, one
  landmark at a time. Low jargon risk; canon consensus > scores.
- **Rock / indie:** lead with scene history and sonic texture. Highest jargon
  risk — this is where the one-term-per-paragraph rule bites.
- **Electronic:** lead with feel and mood, not structure. Always give
  listening *conditions* for ambient ("headphones, lights low").
- **K-pop:** the way in is the era/fandom experience — concept, lore, the
  group's flavor. Sell the world, not the tracklist.
- **Jazz:** mood doorways, not history ("you don't need theory, you don't
  need history — you just need the right doorway in"). Never lead with the
  avant-garde.
- **Film scores:** the way in is the film, or the composer as auteur
  (Hisaishi, Zimmer, Richter). Frame scores as standalone mood records.
