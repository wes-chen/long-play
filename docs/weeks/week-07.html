ANCHOR
ODESZA 2017

A Moment Apart

The record opens with a voice from a 2011 sci-fi film warning that the world is about to end, then spends an hour proving the opposite — that the end of the night is the best part of it.

Verdict: the anchor — the big, bright version of this week's form: every build here pays off in a room full of people singing along.

Scores: 65/100 — Metacritic (the review-aggregator site; 6 critics, 2 positive and 4 mixed — the critics were split). The listeners disagreed: Metacritic's user score is 8.4. The Grammy nominations say more than the 65 — Best Dance/Electronic Album, plus Best Dance Recording for "Line of Sight".

The moment: the Seattle duo's third album, released 8 September 2017, after 2014's In Return — and it landed big: a No. 3 debut on the Billboard 200, their highest charting. Guests run the whole record — Leon Bridges, Regina Spektor, RY X, Naomi Wild, WYNNE, Mansionair — and the duo built it through a dreary Seattle winter, locked in the studio.

Lineage: the widescreen, vocal-led festival electronic that treats a main stage like a choir loft — synths carrying the melody, guest singers carrying the feeling. It is the sound of EDM learning to write songs instead of drops.

Then vs now: critics called it overstuffed at release; the audience made it the duo's commercial peak, and the Grammy nominations made it their calling card. The split between the 65 and the 8.4 is the story — this is a record for the crowd, not the review.

Why you: the week's form in its most direct shape — if you want to hear what a build-and-drop does to a crowd, start with the record built to do it to 20,000 people at once.

LISTEN FOR — Track the build. On 'Line of Sight' and 'Higher Ground', the drop never arrives as a crash — it arrives as a release: layers stack until the kick takes over, then the vocal lands on top. Listen for the exact second the tension tips into payoff.

ADVENTUROUS
JAMIE XX 2015

In Colour

A love letter to London nightlife, written by someone who spent five years trying to get the feeling right. It sounds like the walk home after the club closes, when the night is still ringing in your ears.

Verdict: the adventurous one — the club record that plays like a memory, where the drops are small and the feelings are large.

Scores: 87/100 — Metacritic (the review-aggregator site; 39 reviews, universal acclaim). 9.3/10 — Pitchfork) (the indie review publication; Best New Music). Pitchfork's No. 2 album of 2015 and No. 25 of the 2010s). Mercury Prize and Grammy nominations on top.

The moment: the debut solo album from the xx's producer, released 29 May 2015 on Young Turks), composed over five years while he was still in the band. Guests are the xx's Romy and Oliver Sim, plus Young Thug and Popcaan — and "I Know There's Gonna Be (Good Times)" is built on a sample of The Persuasions' 1971 "Good Times").

Lineage: the patient, melodic side of the London club tradition — UK garage, house, and rave folded into song form. It is closer to a pop record that learned to dance than a dance record that learned to sing.

Then vs now: acclaimed on arrival, and it has only grown — the decade-end lists put it among the best of the 2010s, and it is still the benchmark his later work gets measured against.

Why you: the week's technique at its most restrained — proof that a drop can be a single voice arriving, not a wall of sound.

LISTEN FOR — Listen for the drop that isn't one. On 'Gosh' and 'Loud Places', the beat cuts out and the song keeps moving on voice and steel drums alone — the release comes from what disappears, not what arrives.

DISCLOSURE 2013

Settle

Two brothers from Reigate made the record that turned UK garage into pop's main event — and they did it by letting singers carry songs the beats could have carried alone.

Verdict: the adventurous one — the house record that plays like a pop album, and the week's clearest lesson in letting a vocal do the dropping.

Scores: 81/100 — Metacritic (the review-aggregator site; 31 reviews, universal acclaim). 9.1/10 — Pitchfork.html) (the indie review publication). A No. 1 debut on the UK Albums Chart, a Mercury Prize nomination, and a Grammy nomination for Best Dance/Electronic Album.

The moment: the debut from Guy and Howard Lawrence, released in June 2013 on PMR/Island, built from a run of singles that had already taken over British radio. "Latch" introduced Sam Smith's voice to the world.html); "White Noise" hit No. 2 in the UK — and the album went platinum in the same year.

Lineage: the swung, soulful UK garage tradition — 2-step rhythms and R&B vocals — rebuilt for a generation that met dance music through pop radio. It is the sound of garage coming back as the main event, not the underground.

Then vs now: on release it was the sound of 2013; a decade on, Rolling Stone ranked it the 93rd best debut album of all time.html) — the singles outlived the moment, and the moment turned out to be a movement.

Why you: the week's form with the clearest cause and effect — every track here teaches the build by making you wait for a voice, then paying you off with one.

LISTEN FOR — Follow the vocal against the bass. On 'Latch' and 'White Noise', the singer holds a long line while the bassline pumps underneath — hear how the kick makes the rest of the track duck and breathe, so the drop is something you feel in your chest before you hear it.

ADVENTUROUS
FOUR TET 2010

There Is Love in You

The quietest record on this week's list, and the one that teaches the lesson best. Kieran Hebden builds songs out of loops so patient they stop sounding like loops, then lets them bloom.

Verdict: the adventurous one — the slow-burn version of build-and-drop, where the drop is a melody arriving, not a beat.

Scores: 82/100 — Metacritic (the review-aggregator site; 30 reviews, 27 positive). 8.6/10 — Pitchfork (the indie review publication). Pitchfork's No. 27 album of 2010 and No. 77 of the 2010s.

The moment: released 26 January 2010 on Domino — his first proper album in five years, and the fifth Four Tet record. He spent the gap DJing, remixing, and releasing singles; the album sounds like all of that folded back into one patient whole.

Lineage: the percussion-driven electronic music that treats house and minimalism as the same language — a record that sounds like walking through a city at dusk, the rhythm keeping pace with your feet. Pitchfork's review said it best: it makes a brisk walk through the city sound good.

Then vs now: it landed as a gentle reset after five years away; fifteen years on it sits on Pitchfork's decade list at No. 77 — the quiet record that outlasted the louder ones around it.

Why you: if the week's technique is sidechain compression — the pump under the kick — this is where you hear it breathing: the loops duck and swell, and the whole record moves like lungs.

LISTEN FOR — Listen for the loop that becomes a song. On 'Love Cry' and 'Sing', a single phrase repeats until it stops sounding like a sample and starts sounding like a chorus — the build is patience, the drop is the moment you stop waiting for one.

WILD CARD
BURIAL 2007

Untrue

The wild card, and the record this whole week is secretly about. Burial made a dance album for the walk home after the dance — beats that skip, voices that melt, and a drop that never comes.

Verdict: the wild card — the anti-drop: everything the week's form promises, withheld, until the withholding becomes the feeling.

Scores: 90/100 — Metacritic (the review-aggregator site; 23 reviews, universal acclaim — the most acclaimed album of 2007, tied)). 8.4/10 — Pitchfork) (the indie review publication). Resident Advisor gave it 5/5).

The moment: released 5 November 2007 on Hyperdub), produced alone in the audio editor Sound Forge, mostly at night — friends singing over the phone, R&B a cappellas stretched and pitched until the words came apart. The producer was anonymous; only after the Mercury Prize nomination in 2008 did he reveal himself as William Bevan).

Lineage: the rain-soaked, late-night side of UK garage — 2-step's swing with the drums half-remembered and the vocals stretched until they sound like weather. A dance record made for the street outside the club, not the floor inside it.

Then vs now: the Mercury nomination sent sales up 1004% in a week; Fact named it the best album of the 2000s); in 2017 Pitchfork called it "the most important electronic album of the century so far"). The record nobody could find became the one everybody cites.

Why you: the week's concept, inverted — if the other four records teach you what a build and a drop do, this one teaches you what happens when the drop never arrives and the song is better for it.

LISTEN FOR — Listen for the beat that never quite lands. On 'Archangel' and 'Near Dark', the drums skip and stutter around a vocal that keeps slipping out of time — the drop here is a ghost: the release never comes, and the tension is the whole point.
