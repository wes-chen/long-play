● LONG-PLAY SYLLABUS
WEEK 01 ● DIGEST

How to listen on purpose

Concept — timbre — tone color: why an 808 kick and a real kick playing the same pattern are different instruments. Same pitch, same rhythm, completely different thing.
Technique — reverb — thousands of tiny reflections smeared into one wash. Reverb is the sound of the room the music is "in" — and most rooms on this week's albums were never real rooms at all.
ANCHOR
PINK FLOYD 1973

The Dark Side of the Moon

Known ground — now hear it like a spatial-audio lab taught you. Every song sits in a different room, and reverb is the set design.

LISTEN FOR — the room each song is "in." "Time" opens in a clock shop the size of a closet, then the band kicks in inside a hangar. Same album, different architecture per track.
TRACK NOTES
  1. 1. Speak to Me0:00
    Heartbeat plus the album's sounds arriving one at a time, all flat and close. Notice how small the room feels before it opens up.
  2. 2. Breathe (In the Air)1:04
    The first real room: slide guitar swimming in reverb over that same heartbeat. Same pulse, completely different air.
  3. 3. On the Run3:53
    No band, just synthesizer — the stereo field becomes the instrument as the sequence pans ear to ear.
  4. 4. Time7:29
    Clocks detonating in what sounds like a closet, then the band lands inside a hangar. Compare this drum room to track 1.
  5. 5. The Great Gig in the Sky14:32
    Voice as pure timbre, no words — the reverb tail is doing half the emotional work.
  6. 6. Money19:17
    Cash registers keeping odd meter; the sax sits bone-dry against everything else. That's a mixing decision, not an accident.
  7. 7. Us and Them25:37
    The softest room on the record — two voices stacked until they blur into one thick one.
  8. 8. Any Colour You Like33:29
    The keyboard tone keeps morphing while the notes barely change. Timbre carrying a whole track.
  9. 9. Brain Damage36:55
    Laughter circling your head in stereo; the acoustic guitar underneath is the driest thing on the album.
  10. 10. Eclipse40:46
    Everything returns at once, then the heartbeat from track 1 closes the loop.

ADVENTUROUS
PINK FLOYD 1977

Animals

Same band, colder rooms. The sheep don't sound like the dogs because of the notes — they sound different because of the tone.

LISTEN FOR — how much bigger and emptier the guitars sound than Dark Side — timbre doing narrative work.
TRACK NOTES
  1. 1. Pigs on the Wing 10:00
    Just voice and acoustic guitar, close-miked and dry. The whole album gets colder and bigger from here.
  2. 2. Dogs1:24
    The long one — guitars enormous and empty next to Dark Side. Timbre doing narrative work, as promised.
  3. 3. Pigs (Three Different Ones)18:29
    Talkbox guitar that sounds like it's mocking you; notice how the room shrinks when the funk section hits.
  4. 4. Sheep29:55
    The sheep sounds are the joke and the point — processed voices as instruments, not effects.
  5. 5. Pigs on the Wing 240:14
    The bookend, same close room as track 1. After all that space, dryness feels like shelter.

ADVENTUROUS
THE SMILE 2022

A Light for Attracting Attention

Yorke / Greenwood / Skinner. Compare its drums bar-for-bar against any 808 on Four Tet's record — same job (keep time), different species.

LISTEN FOR — the drums. Real kick, real room, no samples.
TRACK NOTES
  1. 1. The Same0:00
    Strings sawing against a lurching groove. Compare this drum sound bar-for-bar with any 808 this week.
  2. 2. The Opposite4:19
    The bassline does the singing; the drums stay skeletal so every hit lands.
  3. 3. You Will Never Work in Television Again7:25
    The loud one: real kit, real room, no samples. The week's thesis in two minutes.
  4. 4. Pana-vision10:13
    Piano-led and spacious — the reverb here is a room you could actually stand in.
  5. 5. The Smoke14:22
    The groove is all hi-hat and bass; Yorke's voice sits on top like another percussion element.
  6. 6. Speech Bubbles18:01
    The softest track — brushed drums and strings, everything breathing.
  7. 7. Thin Thing22:18
    Greenwood's guitar tone does the talking: jagged, dry, close.
  8. 8. Open the Floodgates26:49
    Ballad with orchestral weight; the piano reverb stretches for days.
  9. 9. Free in the Knowledge31:18
    Acoustic and exposed — you can hear the room around the guitar.
  10. 10. A Hairdryer35:31
    The rhythm stutters and reforms; listen for the kit falling apart and snapping back.
  11. 11. Waving a White Flag40:49
    Motorik pulse, relentless — the timbre barely changes and that's the engine.
  12. 12. We Don't Know What Tomorrow Brings44:36
    Punk brevity after all that sprawl; drums mixed like a garage.
  13. 13. Skrting on the Surface47:53
    The comedown: piano and strings, everything receding into reverb.

ADVENTUROUS
FOUR TET 2013

Beautiful Rewind

Pirate-radio textures, chopped voices, heavy low end. Dozens of tiny samples that have no business sharing a track, placed inside one smeared imaginary space so they sound like they belong together.

LISTEN FOR — reverb as glue — one smeared, imaginary space holding all the fragments.
TRACK NOTES
  1. 1. Gong0:00
    A struck-metal shimmer that never fully decays — the whole album lives inside that smear.
  2. 2. Parallel Jalebi3:14
    Chopped voices folded into the low end; dozens of fragments sharing one imaginary space.
  3. 3. Our Navigation7:08
    The bass does the navigating — reverb gluing samples that have no business coexisting.
  4. 4. Ba Teaches Yoga11:02
    Breathy and looped; the room is entirely synthetic and it doesn't matter.
  5. 5. Kool FM14:22
    Pirate-radio static as texture — the hiss is an instrument, not a flaw.
  6. 6. Crush19:31
    Short burst of pressure; the kick hits harder because everything around it is smeared.
  7. 7. Buchla21:57
    Named for the synth, and it shows — alien tones tamed by that same gluey reverb.
  8. 8. Aerial26:09
    The most open track; fragments drift further apart before the low end pulls them back.
  9. 9. Ever Never32:06
    A thirty-second palate cleanser. Blink and it's gone.
  10. 10. Unicorn32:37
    Voices pitched and stretched until they're pure texture — meaning dissolves, timbre remains.
  11. 11. Your Body Feels36:08
    The comedown groove; the reverb finally relaxes and lets the beat sit dry.

WILD CARD
CAN 1971

Tago Mago

The stretch. A Cologne basement, a Japanese busker on vocals, twenty minutes of "Halleluwah" on one chord. Hypnosis through refusal to vary — if it bounces off you, that's data: note where you check out.

LISTEN FOR — Jaki Liebezeit's hi-hat — the timbre barely changes for the whole album, and that's the point.
TRACK NOTES
  1. 1. Paperhouse0:00
    The welcome mat — an actual song, with Jaki's hi-hat already refusing to vary.
  2. 2. Mushroom7:28
    Damo Suzuki chanting over one chord; the timbre barely moves and the tension builds anyway.
  3. 3. Oh Yeah11:32
    Tapes reversed, voices smeared — the drums stay stubbornly dry underneath the weirdness.
  4. 4. Halleluhwah18:54
    The long one on a single chord. This is the hypnosis test — note exactly when you check out; that's data.
  5. 5. Aumgn37:26
    The basement tape: grunts, static, no beat. The hi-hat's absence is deafening after track 4.
  6. 6. Peking O54:57
    Chaos reassembles into a groove; the drums sound like they're in a concrete room.
  7. 7. Bring Me Coffee or Tea66:41
    The gentle landing — after an hour of refusal-to-vary, a simple song feels radical.
