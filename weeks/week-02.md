# Week 2 — The album as a designed object

**Objective:** hear sequencing — why this song follows that one

**Concept — groove and the pocket.** rhythm is the pattern on paper; groove is humans agreeing how far behind the beat to sit

**Technique — panning/stereo image.** where each sound sits left-to-right; headphones required.

---

## Anchor — Radiohead — *OK Computer*

**Listen for:** The pocket is built, not found — on ‘Airbag’ the drums are chopped and reassembled to land just behind where your body expects; compare that designed lateness to the live-band push of ‘Electioneering.’

## Adventurous — The Beatles — *Abbey Road*

**Listen for:** Ringo’s pocket: on ‘Come Together’ the drums sit so far behind the beat they feel lazy but never drag — that agreed lateness is the week’s concept, and this is the first Beatles album never issued in mono, so the stereo field is the only field.

## Adventurous — Kendrick Lamar — *To Pimp a Butterfly*

**Listen for:** Live, not programmed: ‘The Blacker the Berry’ runs on real drums — hear the push-and-pull against the bass that no grid would allow, then stack it against the machine-locked strut of ‘King Kunta.’

## Adventurous — SZA — *SOS*

**Listen for:** Vocal stacks as architecture — the backing voices on ‘Snooze’ are tracked wide and mixed into a carved pocket; map where each voice lives left-to-right, then hunt the buried Lizzo harmony hidden in ‘F2F.’

## Wild card — Swans — *To Be Kind*

**Listen for:** Repetition as groove: ‘Screen Shot’ hammers one idea until the pocket deepens instead of developing — the repetition is the content, and the groove becomes ritual.
