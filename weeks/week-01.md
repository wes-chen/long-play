# Week 1 — How to listen on purpose

**Objective:** learn the course's core move — one close-listening assignment
per album. This week the assignment is the same every time: pick one sound and
follow it like it's the only instrument in the room.

**Concept — timbre.** Tone color: why an 808 kick and a real kick playing the
same pattern are different instruments. Same pitch, same rhythm, completely
different *thing*.

**Technique — reverb.** Thousands of tiny reflections smeared into one wash.
Reverb is the sound of the room the music is "in" — and most rooms on this
week's albums were never real rooms at all.

---

## Anchor — Pink Floyd — *The Dark Side of the Moon* (1973)

Known ground. You've heard this in a spatial-audio lab; now hear it like the
spat lab taught you. **Listen for:** the room each song is "in." "Time" opens
in a clock shop the size of a closet, then the band kicks in inside a hangar.
Same album, different architecture per track — reverb as set design.

## Adventurous — Pink Floyd — *Animals* (1977)

Same band, colder rooms. **Listen for:** how much *bigger* and emptier the
guitars sound than Dark Side — that's timbre doing narrative work. The sheep
don't sound like the dogs because of the notes; they sound different because
of the tone.

## Adventurous — The Smile — *A Light for Attracting Attention* (2022)

Yorke/Greenwood/Skinner, recorded mostly live to tape. **Listen for:** the
drums. Real kick, real room, no samples — compare its timbre bar-for-bar
against any 808 on Four Tet's record below. Same job (keep time), different
species.

## Adventurous — Four Tet — *Beautiful Rewind* (2013)

Pirate-radio textures, chopped voices, heavy low end. **Listen for:** reverb
as glue — dozens of tiny samples that have no business sharing a track, all
placed inside one smeared, imaginary space so they sound like they belong
together.

## Wild card — Can — *Tago Mago* (1971)

The stretch. A Cologne basement, a Japanese busker on vocals, twenty minutes
of "Halleluwah" on one chord. **Listen for:** Jaki Liebezeit's hi-hat — the
timbre barely changes for the whole album, and that's the point. Hypnosis
through refusal to vary. If it bounces off you, that's data: note *where* you
checked out.
